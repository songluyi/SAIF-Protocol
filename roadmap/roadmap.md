# SAIF Roadmap

The SAIF roadmap develops authorized economic infrastructure in stages. Each phase preserves the same foundational model: a human or company remains the owner, an AI agent or robot acts as an authorized executor, and SAIF enforces identity, permissions, budgets, risk rules, execution controls, and auditability.

## Phase 0: Vision and Protocol Definition

**Objective:** Establish the positioning and protocol foundation for AI Authorized Economic Infrastructure.

Planned outcomes:

- publish the SAIF vision and problem definition;
- define the Authorized Agent Economic Model;
- define Agent Financial Identity v0.1.x;
- specify owner, authorization, budget, risk, transaction, and audit concepts;
- document the reference architecture and trust boundaries; and
- collect use cases for individuals, enterprises, and autonomous machines.

Exit signal: a reviewable protocol foundation with example identity, delegation, and execution models.

## Phase 1: AI Agent Authorization Sandbox

**Objective:** Test delegated economic permissions without exposing real funds or production credentials.

Planned outcomes:

- issue sandbox owner and agent identities;
- create, update, suspend, and revoke authorization scopes;
- simulate category, merchant, transaction, and period limits;
- test human approval challenges and exception handling;
- model spending accounts, reservations, and denials; and
- publish authorization conformance tests and audit traces.

Exit signal: developers can demonstrate that an agent executes only owner-approved economic actions under controlled test conditions.

## Phase 2: MCP Financial Connector

**Objective:** Connect authorized agents to financial and commercial services through policy-controlled MCP tools.

Planned outcomes:

- define a standard MCP economic connector interface;
- implement identity-aware quote, order, budget, payment, and receipt tools;
- isolate provider credentials from agent context;
- verify authorization on every economic tool call;
- connect sandbox merchant, subscription, payment, and accounting services; and
- emit structured execution and audit events.

Exit signal: an agent can complete an end-to-end sandbox purchase through an MCP connector with verifiable owner authorization.

## Phase 3: Enterprise AI Employee Finance

**Objective:** Govern economic activity for large fleets of enterprise AI employees.

Planned outcomes:

- integrate enterprise identity and access management;
- support teams, roles, cost centers, budgets, and approval chains;
- manage thousands of agent identities and delegated permissions;
- connect procurement, treasury, subscription, tax, and accounting workflows;
- provide monitoring, reconciliation, compliance controls, and audit exports; and
- pilot bounded production use cases with explicit ownership and accountability.

Exit signal: an enterprise can operate AI employee spending workflows with precise controls and complete audit trails.

## Phase 4: Autonomous Robot Commerce Infrastructure

**Objective:** Enable robots and autonomous machines to acquire approved products and services within owner-defined operating policies.

Planned outcomes:

- support identities for robots, vehicles, devices, and industrial systems;
- enable authorized purchasing of energy, parts, connectivity, and maintenance;
- connect machine service providers and commerce networks;
- support dynamic operational budgets and risk-based escalation;
- develop machine-to-machine quoting and fulfillment protocols; and
- establish safety, dispute, accountability, and interoperability requirements.

Exit signal: an autonomous machine can request, purchase, and reconcile an approved service while remaining linked to its owner, authorization, budget, and audit record.

## Guiding Principles

- AI is the authorized executor, not the legal or economic owner.
- Controlled delegation comes before broad autonomy.
- Every economic action must be attributable to an owner and agent.
- Authorization, budget, risk, execution, and audit form one control chain.
- Private keys and unrestricted provider credentials remain outside agent context.
- Revocation, recovery, and owner oversight are first-class capabilities.
- Real economic value is introduced only after sandbox safety and conformance criteria are met.

The roadmap is directional and will evolve through implementation evidence, security research, legal review, provider requirements, and community feedback.
