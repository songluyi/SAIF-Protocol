# Authorized Agent Economic Model

## Overview

The Authorized Agent Economic Model defines how an AI agent or autonomous machine can perform economic activities on behalf of a human or organization.

The model does not treat the AI as an independent legal owner. It treats the AI as a software or machine executor whose authority originates from a natural person or legal entity.

## Traditional Model

```text
Human
  ↓
Manual Action
  ↓
Transaction
```

In the traditional model, the human both decides and executes. The person selects the product or service, presents credentials, approves the payment, and retains the transaction record.

This model provides direct accountability but requires human attention for every economic action.

## Future Model

```text
Human / Company
       ↓
Authorization
       ↓
AI Agent
       ↓
SAIF
       ↓
Transaction
```

In the future model, the human or company defines intent and authority. The AI agent performs a task within that authority. SAIF verifies the request, enforces policy and budget controls, executes through connected commerce infrastructure, and records the outcome.

## Core Definition

**The AI Agent is not the owner.**

The funds, account relationships, commercial rights, and legal responsibilities remain associated with the authorizing human or organization.

**The AI Agent is the authorized executor.**

The agent may initiate only the actions allowed by a valid delegation. It cannot expand its own authority, change its own budget, or remove required approvals.

## Roles

### Human or Company Owner

The owner:

- establishes the account and funding relationship;
- creates or approves the agent identity;
- grants and revokes authorization;
- defines budgets, categories, and risk rules;
- receives reports and exception requests; and
- remains accountable for authorized economic activity.

### AI Agent or Robot

The agent:

- acts under a verifiable identity representation;
- interprets an assigned goal;
- prepares an economic intent;
- presents its authorization to SAIF;
- responds safely to denial or approval requirements; and
- does not directly control unrestricted owner credentials.

### SAIF Infrastructure

SAIF:

- verifies owner and agent identity references;
- evaluates authorization, budget, and risk policy;
- obtains additional approval when required;
- routes approved requests to commerce and payment systems;
- prevents out-of-scope execution; and
- records authorization and transaction evidence for audit.

### Commerce or Service Provider

The provider:

- receives a validated commercial request;
- supplies a quote, product, subscription, or service;
- returns order and fulfillment state;
- participates in payment or settlement; and
- provides receipts and dispute references.

## Authorization Lifecycle

1. **Verify owner:** establish the natural person or legal entity responsible for the delegation.
2. **Register agent:** create an identity representation linked to that owner.
3. **Define authority:** specify allowed categories, actions, limits, time windows, and approval rules.
4. **Assign budget:** make a controlled spending capability available to the agent.
5. **Request action:** the agent submits an economic intent and authorization proof.
6. **Evaluate policy:** SAIF checks identity status, scope, budget, risk, and approvals.
7. **Execute or deny:** only an approved request reaches the commerce network.
8. **Record outcome:** SAIF links the authorization decision, agent action, transaction, and receipt.
9. **Review or revoke:** the owner monitors activity and can change or remove authority.

## Example Delegations

- A person authorizes a home agent to purchase household supplies up to a monthly limit.
- A company authorizes an engineering agent to renew approved software subscriptions.
- A procurement agent may request quotes but requires a human to approve every order.
- A warehouse robot may purchase maintenance service from approved vendors within a service budget.
- An autonomous vehicle may pay for charging but cannot purchase unrelated goods.

## Accountability Record

Every completed or denied action should make it possible to answer:

- Who is the accountable owner?
- Which AI agent or robot acted?
- What did the owner authorize?
- Which budget and risk rules applied?
- Was additional approval required and obtained?
- What commercial and financial outcome occurred?
- Which records support reconciliation or dispute resolution?

This chain of evidence is the foundation of authorized agent economics.
